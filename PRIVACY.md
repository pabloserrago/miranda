# Privacy Policy

**Miranda** is built by Pablo Serrano. This policy explains what data the app collects and how it is used.

## The Short Version

Your notes never leave your device. Miranda sends two things to its backend, and neither can be traced back to you: anonymous usage events, and feedback you deliberately choose to submit.

## Data Collection

**Anonymous usage events.** Miranda records when certain things happen in the app — the app was opened, a note was created, a priority was completed — to understand which features are actually used. Each event carries the event name, the time it happened, the app version, and your device language. A few events add non-identifying detail: the hour of day, the day of the week, whether a note included an emoji, and how many minutes a priority took to complete.

These events contain **no identifier of any kind** — no account, no device ID, no random installation ID. There is no value stored alongside them that could group one person's events together or link them to you. **No note content is ever included.**

**Feedback.** If you open "Submit a Request" in Settings and tap Submit, Miranda sends the category you picked, the message you wrote, the app version, and your device language. Nothing is sent unless you tap Submit. The message is free text, so please avoid typing anything into it you would not want to share — it is stored exactly as written.

**Counters shown in the app** (such as how many notes you have created or completed) are computed and stored **locally on your device** only.

## What We Do NOT Collect

- No personal information (name, email, phone, address)
- No accounts — Miranda has no sign-in
- No device identifiers (IDFA, IDFV, or fingerprinting)
- No location data
- No contacts, photos, or files
- No tracking across apps or websites
- No advertising, and no data sold or shared with data brokers
- No third-party analytics SDKs (no Google Analytics, no Firebase, no Mixpanel)

## Notes & Content

All notes you create in Miranda are stored **locally on your device** and in a shared App Group container for the widget. Your notes are never sent to any server.

## Microphone & Dictation

Miranda requests microphone access only when you use voice input to capture notes. Audio is transcribed exclusively by Apple's **on-device** speech recognition — the app requires on-device processing, and if it is not available for your language or device, dictation is disabled rather than using a cloud service. Miranda does not record, store, or transmit audio.

## Artificial Intelligence

Miranda does **not** use any third-party AI service. No note content, audio, transcripts, or any other user data is ever sent to an AI provider. Features such as voice transcription (Apple on-device speech recognition) and text suggestions run entirely on your device using logic bundled with the app.

## Third-Party Services

The usage events and feedback described above are stored in **Supabase**, a hosted database provider, on servers in the **European Union**. Supabase acts purely as a processor — the data is ours, and it is not used for advertising or shared with anyone.

Miranda talks to Supabase over a direct HTTPS request with no SDK embedded in the app. As with any internet request, your IP address is visible to the server and may appear briefly in the provider's operational logs. Miranda does not store your IP address, and it is not part of the usage or feedback records themselves.

Miranda uses no advertising, tracking, or AI services of any kind.

## Retention and Your Rights

Feedback is kept until the request it describes has been dealt with. Usage events are kept in aggregate to track trends over time.

Because the usage events carry no identifier, they cannot be traced back to you — which also means we cannot locate or delete "your" events on request, as there is no way to tell which ones are yours. If you have submitted feedback and would like it removed, email the address below and describe the message; we will delete it.

## Children's Privacy

Miranda does not knowingly collect data from children under 13.
